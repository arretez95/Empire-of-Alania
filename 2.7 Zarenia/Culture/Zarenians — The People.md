Zarenians are a hardy bunch. Living in such a mountainous area has given them robust and agile bodies, and they are no strangers to hard physical labor. Having developed in such an isolated area, Zarenians are a skittish bunch and are mistrusting of outsiders. After the occupation of the Zarenian peninsula by Alanuan forces, their timidness became unbridled hostility.
### History
Zarenians historically have never had a standing army. Their policy was to "Leave and let be". Thus, their method of 'government' was to rely on the most experienced person at hand depending on the task. When Alanuans discovered the allure of Zarenian minerals, they chose to take the land for themselves and did so easily. The ragtag group of farmers, miners, and stonemasons were at a heavy disadvantage over [[The Guardians of Alanue|warmongerers]].

Alanuans exported Zarenian wares by the shipload. Stolen lifestones, bits and pieces of ancient temples, and centuries old religious statues all went on display at the Alanuan royal palace. As their cultural beacons vanished under the manhunt, disquieted Zarenians banded together to overthrow their new rulers. The First Revolt was crushed just as easily as the Conquering. *Sidenote: Zarenians are not very creative with names*.

In an attempt to get the unruly Zarenians under their control (unruly only because Zarenians never had a central government and cared little for regulation), the Alanuans decreed a blood tax. First-born children were sent to Alanue as laborers and promised good wages. Little did these parents know at the time, they were unlikely to see their children again. This was the start of the Zarenian slave period.

When Zarenians discovered the truth and began refusing to pay tribute, Alanuans enforced a blood tax. Couples who wanted marriage had to have a governmental witness they had consummated their marriage. This caused a second revolt, which after its suppression, the aggressors were put to death.

Alanuans eventually passed a law declaring that all female offspring born to a Zarenian mother belonged to the state. Should any harm, abuse, or neglect befall female offspring, the male children will be captured and sold. To be released from this status, the child at age 16 must be partnered with an approved stud. The offspring from this pairing is given up in exchange for her freedom. If the child does not or cannot produce her blood tax by aged 18, she will be sold for labor.

In the current day, the Promise of Freedom group has gained enough traction to pose a threat. The rebel group sabotages Alanuan supply ships, ambushes Guardian patrols, and various other guerilla tactics to make them miserable. The Promise has managed to push the Alanuans back toward the coasts. As of now, Alanuans are only allowed in the port-towns of Casbar and Dunsterton.
### Naming Traditions
Native Zarenians previously used no family names. The practice was introduced during the Conquest. Zarenians take family names from the surrounding nature of where they live, ex: Briar, Tassel, Pines, Maple, etc, or come up with a new name altogether. Freemen returning to their cultural homeland either use the Alanuan House they were born into or forgo a family name altogether.

Feminine names generally use softer consonants and can be shortened. Examples include Amanda, Metis, Melody, Samantha, etc.
Masculine names have more hard consonants and do not shorten as easily: Brandon, Atticus, Ben, Eric, etc.
### Language Groups and Dialects
Zarenians use two different dialects. 

**Mountainspeak** refers to those in the mountain villages. Flatlanders find the dialect slow and harsh. Mountainspeak tends to draw out vowels with hard emphasis on consonants.

**Flatlander** refers to the language spoken in the ports of Casbar and Dunsterton as well as Redcker. It evolves more frequently than Mountainspeak due to frequent encounters with outsiders.
Both are mutually intelligible however as traders and villagers need to move cargo between the ports to the mountains.
Interestingly enough, Zarenians (mainly Mountainspeakers) have a method to communicate and gesture with their feet. As many mountain villagers and merchants lug their merchandise on their persons—carts make it difficult to navigate mountains—they find it easier to balance their wares using their hands, shifting and tapping their feet to communicate:
- *toe, toe, heel* — unsure, undecided, don't know
- *toe, toe, switch heel* — disagree, disapprove, definitely not.
- *stomp* — yes, absolutely, impatience
### Shared Customs and Values
Due to Alanuan intervention in Zarenian family lines where people were forcibly created, separated, or sold into slavery, Zarenians place less value in blood lineage. Adoption is widely accepted, especially in sympathy for war orphans or freed slave children with nowhere to go.

The return of former Zarenian slaves (now called freedmen) brought a large population of unskilled workers, widespread use of illicit drugs, and severe mental trauma. To combat this, the [[The Council of Seven|Council]] passed the Recovery Act which:
1. decriminalized sex work and the possession of illicit drugs, both prohibited during the Occupation. The Council believed resources were better suited to preventing or reducing addiction instead of forcing a large demographic into prison.
2. established a strict penalty (all assets seized by the government, a substantial fine, and lifetime imprisonment) for those caught supplying illicit substances.
3. provided healthcare and counseling to citizens. Hospitals installed drug stations where addicted persons can receive doses of drugs. Strict recordkeeping is kept on those who have been visiting for services. 

Many criticized the Recovery Act, claiming this was the government trying to drug the new nation into submission. 

Notes:
- Zarenia is the first nation to recognize mental health as the source of calamity. 
- Along with this were projects to expand the ports of Casbar and Dunsterton for increased commerce and provide work for the people 
### Art
#### Music
Zarenians are extremely fond of percussive sounds. If something makes a noise, they will make an instrument out of it. That includes themselves. Tap dancing, body slapping, vocal percussion, acapella bands, etc. are fond Zarenian past times.
#### Art
Zarenians are familiar with basic crafts—weaving, woodworking, etc—but are most well-known for their stone carvings. Everything from intricate beadwork to exquisite sculptures can be found scattered throughout Zarenia. Sculptures often depict things of nature, most commonly trees. The most famous statue—a rendering in the image of the Creator Dragon Zalen—guards the gates of Casbar, acting as the first landmark to arrivals. 
### Food & Cuisine
The most important crops in Zarenia are potatoes, barley, and oats, and Zarenians eat a lot of all three.
### Customs and Traditions
#### New Moon Festival
The celebration of the first full moon of the year. In ancient times, Zarenians called the moon the Eye of Zalen. Each lunar cycle was Zalen's eye opening and closing. The New Moon Festival was meant to offer thanks for his protection. Now it's become a festival to welcome the new year.
#### CELEBRATION OF THE WIND
This holiday stretches three days long for people to rest and recover from their work. On the day of the summer solstice, people go around gathering flowers and herbs to sprinkle on each other's doorsteps.
#### WINTER SOLSTICE
The winter solstice is the time people finish harvests of their winter crops. Everyone receives a week of rest after the harvest day. During this time, they partake in hot soups, cakes, and other sweet foods.
#### THE POTATO FESTIVAL
Daniel and his father initially started this feast as a joke after the family grew too many potatoes and ran out of storage. They set up a few tables in Reasal's town square with a variety of potatoes carved and cooked in weird ways. Eventually, people started showing up with their own potato carvings and it became a contest of who could carve the weirdest potato. For the past 15 years, after the yearly potato harvest, people get together for the Weirdest Potato Carving Contest in which food, drink, and laughter are automatically provided.
