The universe was a massive game to the gods. The game was simple. Build something. Some gods grouped together and built wondrous things. The Milky Way, the Andromeda Galaxy... Some gods competed to see who could build the biggest stars before they blew up. Other gods built things to build things. One god created rocks with such glorious colors, many other gods grew envious.

Plasma was created while the gods busily played their games, building their universe and pitting their creations against each other. Born from the remains of abandoned projects, It was filled with bitterness and envy of the emptiness of those forsaken stars. As the gods poured love and effort into their ongoing efforts, Its emptiness only grew until It felt only the need for destruction. It was going to be the light that caught everybody's attention.

But one day, as It wandered, Its destructive force spewing forth, It came across the young God of the Blue Essence. He had created something He called Life! It watched, amazed, as his beloved creations lowered themselves to the ground at his Holiness. Its heart dropped as they destroyed each other, all for their love of this one God. Who loved him more? And who loved him the best? It wasn't unlike these mortal beings, despite the vast difference in lifespan. They were filled with such dire need for love, and so was It. And Plasma wondered: whatever created It, did they love It as much as this young God loved his people? A tear fell from Its eye, the shining drop enveloping the planet in radiant color.

Plasma found It could look into the minds of the organisms and glean any information It so desired, from the smallest ant to the largest whale. It sensed their need for survival, to find resources, to find and continue Life. Each network between organism was so complex and convoluted, Plasma wasn't sure how the young God had built this little blue rock. But it was absolutely gorgeous how green trees contrasted beautifully against indigo sky, how different animals optimized their abilities to suit their surroundings. This was not like anything It had ever seen. And it certainly wasn't like the rest of the universe, all dustclouds, gases, and stars It had lit afire.

The young God was so happy to find someone who truly appreciated his creations. The other gods, still resentful of not being the first to develop this oceanic blue marble, did their best to destroy it. Asteroids, meteors, solar flares, all descended upon this little blue stone. And Plasma took it upon Itself to defend it. It used as much plasma as this little blue stone could hold, allowing it to permeate the atmosphere and burn off most of the harmful particles. The young God helped to fend off the rest of the attacks. Not knowing when the next assault would be, Plasma swore to its new friend that It would help protect his little blue stone. It would see that he and his creations had everything they needed.

Plasma discovered quickly the instability of its powers on Little Blue. The environment and atmosphere were so dynamic it was difficult to stay in one place. With help from the young God, Plasma divided Itself into the form of mythical beasts, which the humans refer to the Creators, coming to believe it was from they that humanity was created.

The first was Zalen, the Earth Dragon of Creation. He was responsible for nurturing Little Blue's newest lifeforms. He created land for all beings to live upon.

The second was Aeon, the Water Serpent of Life. She was tasked with safeguarding Little Blue's health. To ensure it never thirsted, to quench the fires of war, she kept the world's basins filled with water.

The third was Cepher, the Wind Rukh of Equilibrium. She maintained the balance of natural energy. She brought rain to regions in drought and soil to areas lacking farmland.

The fourth was Entan, Plant Basilisk of Restoration. He oversaw the rejuvenation of regions affected by destruction or disaster, flowers and trees blooming where he stepped.

The last was Furos, the Fire Salamander of Vitality. She ensured that the cycle of life remained constant, that nutrient and nourishment remained available to all.

They taught the people the powers of nature (link to natural energy) to care for themselves and others, to resolve conflicts without harm, and ushered in a millennium of peace.

Until one Creator disappeared. The Creators found themselves dragged into the conflict as accusations flew with no answers for Cepher's evanescence. Who would bring the rain from Aeon's oceans? Who would scatter seeds from Entan's flowers? Who would bring Zalen's nourishment to those in need, and who would bring Furos' fire to places overgrown?

In outrage, Cepher's acolytes declared war on Furos, who they believed to be a god of destruction. Furos' claim of innocence only enraged them further.

The world fell into war, humans taking sides against or with the Creators. The Creators tried to quell the violence and pacify their followers to no avail. Furos, angered by her contemporaries' lack of support, turned on them and burned everything they had built. Aeon put out Furos' fires. Entan was furious with both of them for burning and drowning his thriving flora. Zalen had complaints that the war was taking its toll on his soil. So too, did the Creators engage in the chaos.

Some say the war still rages, Aeon's waves battering on the shore. Entan's flowers still push up from even the most barren of grounds. Zalen remains on defense, holding off the encroachers. Furos wants nothing to do with any of them, keeping herself high in the sky.

Even though their arts of magic were lost long ago, and their presence intermittent, their energy still seeps through the land in hopes they can return to nourish it.
